# Compliance notes (not legal advice)

- **GDPR (UK/EU):** names, emails and headlines are personal data. Write a legitimate-interest assessment; give an
  Art. 14 notice at first contact or on request (where the data came from); honour the right to object at once
  (suppression); keep only what the email needs; set a retention period (bundle suggestion: delete unanswered
  prospects' personal fields after 12 months, assumed).
- **PECR (UK):** cold email to corporate subscribers is allowed without consent. Sole traders and some partnerships
  count as individuals (consent needed). Every email identifies the sender and offers an opt-out.
- **CAN-SPAM (US):** covers B2B. Needs a working opt-out and a postal address (add yours to the signature in
  `kb/voice.md`). The bundle keeps the opt-out line on by default. The author dropped it in his own campaigns as his
  own risk call (2026-10-27); the bundle does not copy that.
- **Bulk-sender rules (Gmail/Yahoo 2024, Outlook 2025):** SPF/DKIM/DMARC, complaints < 0.3%, one-click unsubscribe
  above ~5,000 a day (sourced).
- **Data sources:** HTTP Archive, urlscan public scans and registers under open licences are low risk; check each
  register's licence for marketing use. Check urlscan's free tier for commercial use (assumed). Google Maps ToS bans
  scraping (cap runs, keep data internal). LinkedIn: see source-social/references/compliance.md.
- **UK fines and complaints (Data (Use and Access) Act 2025):** PECR fines rose to up to GBP 17.5m or 4% of turnover;
  messages count as sent even if undelivered; a complaints procedure is required (sourced). The corporate / sole-trader
  rule is unchanged.
- **Other markets:** Ireland is similar to the UK. Germany and Spain effectively require consent even for B2B email, so
  cold email is not a channel there (sourced, general). Check each target country before the plan picks email.
- **Calls and SMS:** see `email-and-call.md` (do-not-call screening, caller ID, SMS consent).
- **Provenance:** keep the source URL for every contact found by research, so "how did you get my email?" has a true
  answer.
