# Copy rules

Sources: the bundle author's rules that held in their campaigns (2026-10/11) and generic cold-email practice
(adapted from the open marketingskills cold-email skill, vendor data, directional). Owner rules in `kb/voice.md` and
`kb/decisions.md` win.

## Email 1
- Plain text. No links, images, attachments or tracking. ≤ 90 words (author's emails: 42–55 words).
- 2–4 word lowercase subject that looks internal. No pitch, no first name, no numbers, no urgency, no emojis,
  no "free" or "guarantee", never a fake "Re:" or "Fwd:".
- First line: one fact about *their* system, checkable today. Personalisation must connect to the problem; a name
  alone is not personalisation.
- Write like a peer: short sentences, contractions, more "you" than "I / we". Don't open with who we are.
- One low-friction ask answerable in one line: a free check or a sample ("Want me to send the note?"). No 30-minute
  call in email 1.
- Every claim true and checkable. Never promise a result we can't give without their data.
- Greeting: `Hi {first name},` else `Hi there,`.

## Follow-ups
- No greeting. Each adds one new point. Never "just following up", "checking in", "bumping this", "I never heard back".
- Same thread (the app sends "Re: <subject>" with threading headers).
- The last email says it is the last, then stop.
- Default timing: day 0, +3 and +5 business days. Generic practice spreads 3–5 emails over growing gaps (0, 3, 7–8,
  optional 14, last 21–28); about 40% of replies come from follow-ups (sourced, vendor). Best Tue–Thu, 9–11 or 13–15
  local time (sourced, vendor).

## Banned
Stock openers ("Quick question", "I hope this email finds you well", "My name is…", "I came across…") · "It's not X,
it's Y" · "no X, no Y, no Z" lists · a question answered by the next words · colon reveals · em dashes · exclamation
marks · jargon (leverage, synergy, unlock, best-in-class, game-changer) · "I saw your comment / like" · tab titles as
the hook · reply-rate or ROI promises.

## Variants
Test one thing at a time (subject, opener, angle or ask), 2–3 variants. At low reply rates a difference needs a few
hundred sends per variant; judge on positive replies. Different copy per segment beats more variants. Vary the copy:
identical bodies across many sends look like bulk mail.

## What didn't work for the author (measured 2026-10-27..11-02)
Pitching what a SaaS under ~$1k a month already covers (clinics, recruiters, DTC): 0 replies. A "signal" the buyer
already knew (public news) is not a hook.
