# Partner and introducer channels (generic playbook)

From generic practice in a lead-generation handbook (sourced, 2026-11) and the bundle author's notes. No figures
here are measured by the author: treat every number as a starting assumption to test.

## Who
- **Hubs** first: a few partners who each reach many buyers (a software reseller network, a tool's implementation
  partner directory, an account-manager network, an industry association). The long tail of occasional introducers
  comes second. Plans that need many signed partners rarely work without hubs (assumed).
- Partners who stand at the moment of the decision (the person who sets up the tool, files the licence, signs the
  lease, does the books) beat partners who only know the buyer.
- Rank candidate partner types by fit: do they meet the buyer before the need, can they earn twice (a referral now and
  a service later), does working with you take work off their plate.

## Mechanics (copy the gates)
- Attribution at first contact (a unique partner code or link), a post-activation gate (pay after the client is live
  or paid), clawback if the client cancels inside the gate, ownership timestamps, a partner-visible status, a payout
  ledger, "partner-sourced" vs "partner-influenced" tags, and a referral-fee register.
- Rewards: a flat fee per live client or a share of the first payment; a first-deal bonus helps activation. Set them
  from your margin, not from competitors' headlines.

## Outreach cadence to partners (assumed, adjust)
About 15 business days, 8 to 9 touches, stop on any reply: profile view → connect → call + email 1 (corporate address)
→ a comment on their post → call 2 + voicemail → message if connected → email 2 with a give-first asset → call 3 →
a last email. Ask for a 15-minute fit call, never a sign-up. Avoid each partner type's busy season.

## Measure from day one
Partners contacted → conversations → first meetings → signed → activated (first referral) within 30 and 90 days →
first won deal → repeat referrer; deals per active partner per month.

## Compliance (not legal advice)
- Professional bodies (for example accountants' institutes) often require partners to disclose commissions and get
  the client's written consent: offer a flat, disclosable fee, the option to pass it to the client, and ready
  disclosure wording.
- Referral emails: the referrer sends a link in their own message and controls what it says. Never hand referrers a
  marketing email to forward (it becomes your marketing under e-privacy rules).
- Anti-bribery: a fee is lawful unless it induces improper performance; keep disclosure clauses, due diligence and a
  register. Referral income is taxable for the recipient; take advice on fee terms.
